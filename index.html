BluePort AI classifies photos of waste into six streams used in port environmental management. A frozen CLIP encoder and a tiny trained head do the work, so it runs on any laptop and is easy to audit.

How it works

No fine-tuning of a large model. CLIP turns the photo into a 512-number embedding; a logistic-regression head trained on that embedding assigns the stream. Training takes seconds and the head is 16 KB.

Photo→CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder (frozen)→512-d embedding→Linear probe, 6 classes→Stream + confidence

Practical

Browser demo on a free Space, or an offline Telegram bot on a laptop where photos never leave the machine.

Limitation

Clean-item photos

Training images are mostly single items on plain backgrounds. Mixed, dirty or partially hidden port waste scores lower; predictions under 50% are flagged.

Limitation

Six streams only

Wood, textiles, oily rags and hazardous cargo residues are not covered yet.

Origin

From river to port

Started in the Omdena Ganges River plastic-interceptor challenge, then adapted to port waste streams within research on port sustainability at UFMA, Brazil.

Six waste streams

Aligned with port environmental management practice and the segregation required for ship-generated waste under MARPOL Annex V.

Plastic

Bottles, bags, packaging, film. Never to be discharged at sea.

Paper and cardboard

Boxes, documents, packaging. Recyclable when clean and dry.

Glass

Bottles and jars. Keep whole where possible; broken glass in a rigid container.

Metal

Cans, cables, scrap. Segregated metal stream; MARPOL Annex V for ship-generated scrap.

Organic

Food waste and plant matter. Compost or organic stream; strict discharge rules at sea.

E-waste

Boards, cables, devices. Licensed WEEE handler, never general waste.
